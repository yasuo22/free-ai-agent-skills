# Pitch Deck

## When to use

- Preparing a fundraising pitch and need a complete story arc that fits in 10 minutes
- An internal project review where you must explain "why this, how, and how much" to leadership
- Competing in a startup competition or Demo Day and need a complete, instantly-understandable version
- You have a first draft of a deck but the logic is scattered, and it needs restructuring through an investor's eyes
- The founder can't articulate the product's value clearly and needs the one-line hook polished first

## Workflow

1. **Gather ammunition (input: founder's notes / existing materials → output: fact list)**
   Get the 6 key facts straight before writing a word: ① what it does in one sentence ② who the target users are and how much the pain hurts ③ product progress (demo / beta / launched + data) ④ market size (TAM / SAM / SOM, all three layers) ⑤ core team members' backgrounds ⑥ how much you're raising this round and how much equity is on offer. Chase down whatever's missing — never substitute hand-waving like "the market is huge" for facts.

2. **Polish the hook (input: fact list → output: one-line positioning + 3 keywords)**
   Write the cover page's one-line positioning in this format: "We're the [product] for [audience], [solving problem] with [approach]." Then distill 3 keywords — the entire deck revolves around those 3 words, and anything off-theme gets cut.

3. **Write the per-slide script (input: hook + fact list → output: 10-slide outline)**
   Follow the 10-slide structure in the output spec. Every slide has: "slide title, the question it answers, up to 3 bullet points, image/data suggestion, script (speakable in 60 seconds)." Write the text draft first; only think about layout once the logic holds together.

4. **Honesty-check the numbers (input: first draft → output: annotated final draft)**
   Go slide by slide and check every number: sourced numbers get their source; unsourced ones become qualitative statements or get deleted; forecasts must be labeled "forecast" with the underlying assumptions; competitive comparisons only state publicly verifiable information.

5. **Layout guidance (input: final draft → output: visual direction checklist)**
   Give per-slide layout guidance: type-size hierarchy, color palette (primary + secondary + accent, max 3 colors), chart suggestions (one chart per slide). No design files — but descriptions concrete enough for a designer or AI image tool to execute directly.

6. **Rehearsal checklist (input: final draft → output: dry-run self-check)**
   Produce a dry-run checklist: is total time ≤ 10 minutes, does every slide have a "so what" (what does this mean for the investor), plus Q&A prep — the 5 most likely questions and answer outlines.

## Output spec

- 10 slides total, fixed order: ① cover ② pain ③ solution ④ product demo ⑤ market size ⑥ business model ⑦ competition ⑧ team ⑨ financials & milestones ⑩ the ask & vision
- Each slide answers one specific investor question, in order: who are you / how much does the pain hurt / why can you solve it / what does the product look like / how big is the market / how do you make money / why do you win / why is this team credible / where the money goes and what it achieves / how much, for what equity, and what the future looks like
- Max 3 bullets per slide, max 20 words per bullet; per-slide script 100–150 words
- Numbers must have units and a time range (e.g. "Q3 2026", not "currently")
- Companion template.md skeleton — fill in the blanks and use it

## Good vs. bad examples

- ❌ Bad pain slide: "As digital transformation accelerates, traditional businesses face many challenges, and the market potential is enormous." (empty talk, no scene, no data)
- ✅ Good pain slide: title "The shop owner losing 2 hours a day to reconciliation"; 3 scenes: ① up at 11pm flipping through paper receipts ② a promo-day miscalculation that cost ¥3,000 ③ can't justify ¥4,000/month for a bookkeeper; data: 73% of small shops still keep books by hand (source: XX association 2025 survey)
- ❌ Bad cover: "XX Smart Tech Co., Ltd. — Making the world a better place"
- ✅ Good cover: "We're the mobile cash register for neighborhood shops — we use receipt photo recognition to fix slow, error-prone manual bookkeeping"
- ❌ Bad ask slide: "Raising ¥10M this round for product R&D and marketing"
- ✅ Good ask slide: "Raising ¥8M for 10%: ¥4M R&D (2 release cycles), ¥3M marketing (cover 500 stores to validate the model), ¥1M team; 18 months to ¥2M monthly revenue"
- Standard: every slide must have at least two of — a real person, a real scene, a real number. Go back and fill in what's missing; adjectives don't count.

## Notes

- Never fabricate data: an unverified market-size number is better written as "to be validated" than made up — this is the quality floor
- One slide, one idea: cramming too much is the most common way pitch decks die; anything you can't fit moves to the appendix
- No product on the pain slide: make the investor feel the pain first, then offer the medicine — get the order backwards and you lose all persuasion power
- The team slide sells "relevance", not "prestige": a big-company title is less convincing than "built a similar product, served similar customers"
- The ask slide must be self-consistent: raise amount, valuation, equity stake, and the 18-month spend plan must all line up with each other
