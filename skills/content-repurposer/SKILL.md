# Content Repurposer

## When to use

- You published a newsletter and want LinkedIn posts, carousel outlines, short-video scripts, and notes from it without writing from scratch
- You have a blog post or whitepaper and need a week's worth of social content that stays faithful to the original
- Your content cadence outpaces your writing time and one solid idea must stretch across 3–4 platforms
- A teammate writes the long-form piece and you need a repeatable way to adapt it per platform
- You're building a content flywheel and need the "one in, many out" step standardized with quality guardrails

## Workflow

1. **Feed the original (input: finished long-form piece → output: source text + fact list)**
   Paste the full newsletter or blog post. Extract the real insights, hooks, claims, and numbers — each tagged with where it appeared in the original. Nothing enters the pipeline without a source to point back to.

2. **Set the voice rules (input: 3–5 of your past pieces → output: voice guide)**
   Collect your own samples that sound like you and write down what makes them yours: sentence length, tone, words you never use. A voice guide can't be handed to you — it has to be built from your writing over time, and it's what keeps rewrites from sounding generic.

3. **Map one-to-many (input: fact list + insights → output: platform plan)**
   Decide which insight goes to which format: carousel outline, 60-second video script, LinkedIn post, short note. Not every insight fits every format — cut before writing, not after.

4. **Rewrite per platform (input: platform plan + voice guide → output: drafts)**
   Rewrite each piece in that platform's native shape: hook-first for video, slide-by-slide for carousels. Keep every fact traceable to the original piece. Flag anything unverified instead of smoothing it over.

5. **Fact-check pass (input: drafts → output: flagged draft set)**
   Verify every number, quote, and claim against the original. Anything that came only from the agent gets flagged or deleted. This step is not optional — one real case let a free agent run without it and shipped fabricated data; only a fixed workflow with per-step guardrails was reliable.

6. **Approve and schedule (input: clean drafts → output: scheduled posts)**
   A human reviews, edits, and approves every piece. Only approved pieces go into the scheduler. Review the flagged items first — they're usually where the agent got creative.

## Output spec

- Per source piece: carousel outline, short-video script(s), LinkedIn post(s), short note(s)
- Each piece carries: source insight, facts used (with origin), target platform, target length
- Flagged-items list: anything unverified, with a proposed resolution (cut / re-source / label as opinion)
- One batch doc per source piece so everything stays traceable
- Companion template.md skeleton — fill in the blanks and use it

## Good vs. bad examples

- ❌ Bad carousel: "AI is changing marketing. Here's why it matters." (generic, no insight, nothing traceable to your newsletter)
- ✅ Good carousel outline: slide 1 hook "I cut my content week from 6 hours to 45 minutes — here's the exact 8-step pipeline"; slides 2–7 each show one step plus the rule that keeps facts honest; slide 8 CTA; every stat cites the newsletter section it came from
- ❌ Bad video script: opens with "Did you know 90% of marketers struggle with content?" — the number came from nowhere; this is exactly how the failed real-world case shipped invented data
- ✅ Good video script: 60-second structure — 0–5s hook quoting the newsletter's own line, 5–40s the three platform rules with the failed-agent story as the cautionary example, 40–60s CTA; zero numbers not present in the source
- ❌ Bad workflow: "Give the agent my newsletter and let it make social posts." (one real case tried this — the agent fabricated statistics)
- ✅ Good workflow: fixed steps (extract → hook → structure → conclusion → tone check) with a guardrail at each step: only facts and numbers that appear in the original
- Standard: every repurposed piece must name the source insight and cite its origin. If you can't trace it, it doesn't ship.

## Notes

- Distilled from real cases of creators turning newsletters into social content — including one case where a free agent fabricated data and only a fixed eight-step workflow with per-step guardrails proved reliable.
- Never let the agent add facts: anything not in the source is flagged or deleted — this is the single most important rule in the whole skill
- A voice guide is a long game: collect your own samples over time; nobody can hand you one that works
- Platform formats shift with algorithms: revisit the format templates when reach drops, not the facts
- Batch by source piece, not by platform: one original per doc keeps everything traceable
